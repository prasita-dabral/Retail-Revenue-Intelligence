# Executive Memo — Retail Revenue Intelligence

## Executive Overview

The 2016 retail transaction analysis identified a significant revenue decline between May and August 2016, followed by a strong recovery in September.

Revenue decreased from $142,719.86 in May to $116,908.98 in August, representing a decline of $25,810.88 (18.1%). Revenue then recovered to $141,239.89 in September, an increase of 20.81% compared with August.

The analysis was performed at category and product level to identify the contributors to these movements and to determine whether changes were primarily associated with sales volume or revenue per unit.

## What Happened?

Revenue increased steadily from January through May before declining for three consecutive months.

The largest decline occurred between May and August.

The five categories with the largest revenue declines were:

1. LPF
2. 1EO
3. J4R
4. 0H2
5. R6E

The decline was therefore concentrated among specific categories rather than being evenly distributed across the product portfolio.

## What Drove the Decline?

The product-level analysis indicates that lower sales volume was an important contributor to the decline.

Several major declining products experienced substantial reductions in units sold between May and August. Some products that generated sales in May recorded no August sales.

Monetization changes were also observed for individual products, but the results indicate that volume deterioration was a major recurring pattern among the identified declining contributors.

## How Did Revenue Recover?

Revenue increased sharply from $116,908.98 in August to $141,239.89 in September.

The strongest recovering categories were:

1. 1EO
2. SJS
3. LPF
4. OXH
5. 1L6

The recovery was associated with increased unit sales across several key products.

Product reactivation also contributed to the recovery, with some products generating September sales after recording no August sales.

## Business Recommendations

### 1. Monitor declining product volume

Products showing substantial reductions in units sold should be monitored closely to identify emerging revenue deterioration.

### 2. Investigate temporary zero-sales periods

Products that move from generating sales to recording zero sales should be investigated using additional operational data to determine the underlying reason.

### 3. Protect strong recovery contributors

Products that contributed strongly to the September recovery should be monitored to determine whether their improved performance can be sustained.

### 4. Monitor volume and monetization separately

Revenue changes should be decomposed into units sold and revenue per unit so that volume-related and monetization-related problems can be addressed differently.

### 5. Establish recurring revenue monitoring

Monthly revenue, units sold, revenue per unit, category performance, and product performance should be monitored regularly to identify deterioration and recovery patterns earlier.

## Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates that transaction-level retail data can be transformed into actionable business intelligence.

By combining revenue trends with category, product, volume, and monetization analysis, the business can move beyond identifying that revenue changed to understanding where the change occurred and which performance factors were associated with it.
