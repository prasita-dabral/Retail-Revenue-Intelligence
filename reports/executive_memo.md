# Executive Memo

### Retail Revenue Decline & Recovery Analysis — 2016

**Prepared by:** Prasita Dabral
**Focus period:** May – September 2016
**Data:** 131,706 point-of-sale line items from 2016 (22,625 customers, 64,682 transactions)

---

## 1. Bottom Line

Revenue fell **18.1%** from May to August, from **$142,720 to $116,909** (−$25,811), then recovered **20.8%** in September to **$141,240**, within 1.0% of May.

The decline was **volume-led**: fewer customers and fewer orders, not smaller baskets. The clearest driver was a **59.6% fall in new customers** (2,626 in May to 1,060 in August).

September restored revenue but not acquisition. New-customer arrivals fell back to about 1.2–1.4K a month in the fourth quarter, and revenue now leans more heavily on returning customers.

---

## 2. Key Numbers

| Metric | May | August | September |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $142,720 | $116,909 | $141,240 |
| Transactions | 6,134 | 4,648 | 5,732 |
| Active customers | 4,418 | 3,303 | 4,089 |
| New customers | 2,626 | 1,060 | 1,625 |
| Average transaction value (ATV) | $23.27 | $25.15 | $24.64 |

Monthly revenue fell 8.0% in June ($131,305), 8.2% in July ($120,592) and 3.1% in August ($116,909).

---

## 3. What Drove the Decline

### 3.1 Volume, not value
Transactions fell 24.2% while ATV rose 8.1%, and revenue per unit rose from $7.80 to $8.41. Part of the ATV rise is a mix effect: new customers spend less per order ($20.70 in May) than returning customers ($26.11), so losing them lifts the average.

### 3.2 New-customer acquisition collapsed
New customers fell from 2,626 to 1,060, and their revenue fell from $66.7K to $27.8K. The customers who did arrive were also slightly less active in their first month: transactions per customer fell from 1.23 to 1.12 and units from 3.23 to 2.82. Revenue per new customer was roughly flat ($25.39 to $26.19), so the loss was almost entirely headcount.

### 3.3 Revenue by customer type
| Customer type | May to August revenue change |
|---|---:|
| New | −$38.9K |
| Continuing | −$8.4K |
| Reactivated | +$21.5K |
| **Net** | **−$25.8K** |

Reactivated customers cushioned the decline. Continuing-customer revenue fell mainly in July and August, as the smaller June and July cohorts flowed through.

### 3.4 Newer cohorts also retained worse
Month-1 retention was 28.2% for the March cohort, 28.8% for April and 23.8% for May, against 17.7% for June, 14.2% for July and 15.4% for August. The problem was both a smaller intake and a weaker repeat rate.

### 3.5 Established customers are far more valuable per head
In May, revenue per active customer was $81.61 for frequent customers, $47.59 for regular and $35.79 for occasional customers, against $25.39 for customers with no pre-May history. However, active customers with pre-May history also fell 27–30% in every tier between May and August.

### 3.6 Categories: broad-based, moderately concentrated
The decline was broad-based but moderately concentrated: 119 of 173 categories with May or August sales declined, while the seven largest decliners (LPF, 1EO, J4R, 0H2, R6E, Q4N and P42) account for about 27% of the gross category decline and the ten largest about 36%.

In LPF, 1EO and J4R, the loss from new customers was the largest component of the drop, partly offset by reactivated customers. Product-level swings are noisy: in the four categories examined in detail, 105 of 190 declining products sold more again in September.

---

## 4. The September Recovery

Transactions rose 23.3% (+1,084) and active customers rose 23.8% (+786), while ATV slipped from $25.15 to $24.64. The recovery was volume-led.

| Customer type | August to September revenue change |
|---|---:|
| New | +$12.5K |
| Continuing | +$8.8K |
| Reactivated | +$3.0K |
| **Net** | **+$24.3K** |

September's customers were 1,625 new, 1,246 continuing and 1,218 reactivated.

---

## 5. Did the Recovery Last?

Share of September's customers still active:

| September group | Customers | October | November | December |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| New | 1,625 | 19.0% | 16.2% | 18.3% |
| Continuing | 1,246 | 60.8% | 58.5% | 58.9% |
| Reactivated | 1,218 | 36.0% | 33.7% | 34.9% |

In revenue terms, new customers kept about 22–27% of their September revenue in October to December, reactivated customers about 43–49%, and continuing customers about 68–76%.

Revenue held through the fourth quarter ($140.9K in October, $132.9K in November, $152.1K in December). But new-customer arrivals fell back to 1,359, 1,185 and 1,202, against 2,200–2,600 a month from February to May, and reactivated customers rose from 11% of May revenue to 34% of December revenue.

**September restored revenue without restoring acquisition.**

---

## 6. Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnose the drop in new-customer acquisition (channels, traffic, conversion, promotions, assortment) | New customers fell 59.6% and never returned to their earlier level |
| 2 | Improve early-life retention, especially first-to-second purchase conversion | Month-1 retention fell to 14–18% in the decline cohorts |
| 3 | Protect continuing customers and run reactivation programs | Continuing customers retained about 59–61%; reactivated customers now supply about a third of revenue |
| 4 | Review the highest-impact declining categories, after testing whether they simply reflect lost new customers | Category declines followed customer inflow, and product-level swings are noisy |
| 5 | Track leading indicators: new customers per month, month-1 retention, reactivated customers, revenue share by customer type | These moved before revenue |

---

## 7. Limitations

- **One year of data.** A seasonal summer dip cannot be separated from a real acquisition problem.
- **"New" means first observed purchase.** The data starts on January 2, 2016, so January customers are not truly new and early months overstate new customers.
- **An unexplained break in cohort behavior.** January and February cohorts fade to about 1%, while every cohort from March on plateaus at 12–24%.
- **No cause data.** There is no marketing, traffic, inventory, pricing or promotion data, so the analysis shows where the shortfall came from, not why.
- **Calendar gaps.** The date table has 363 days. January 1, March 27 and December 25 have no sales, likely closures.

---

## 8. Closing Assessment

The decline was material, and revenue recovered. The weakness behind it, a shrinking and weaker new-customer intake, has not.

The priority is not to reproduce September's revenue but to rebuild the flow that creates it: **acquire customers, retain them early, reactivate lapsed customers and protect established ones**.
