# Key Insights & Recommendations

**Retail Revenue Decline & Recovery Analysis — May to September 2016**

This page summarizes the findings and what to do about them. Tables and method detail are in the [deep-dive analysis](deep_dive_analysis.md), and the one-page version is in the [executive memo](executive_memo.md).

---

## Key Insights

### 1. The decline was a volume problem, not a value problem
Revenue fell 18.1% from May to August ($142,720 to $116,909). Transactions fell 24.2% (6,134 to 4,648), while average transaction value (ATV) rose 8.1% ($23.27 to $25.15).

Part of the ATV rise is a mix effect: new customers spend less per order ($20.70 in May) than returning customers ($26.11), so losing them lifts the average.

### 2. New-customer acquisition was the clearest driver
New customers fell 59.6%, from 2,626 in May to 1,060 in August, and their revenue fell from $66.7K to $27.8K. The drop was a step change, not a drift: arrivals dropped from 2,626 to 1,569 in June alone. Revenue per new customer was roughly flat ($25.39 to $26.19), so the loss was almost entirely headcount.

### 3. Newer cohorts also retained worse
Month-1 retention was 28.2% for the March cohort, 28.8% for April and 23.8% for May, against 17.7% for June, 14.2% for July and 15.4% for August. The business acquired fewer customers, and fewer of them came back.

### 4. The loss was broad-based, with moderate concentration
119 of 173 categories with May or August sales declined. The seven largest decliners (LPF, 1EO, J4R, 0H2, R6E, Q4N, P42) account for about 27% of the gross category decline, and the ten largest about 36%.

In LPF, 1EO and J4R, lost new customers were the largest single component of the drop, partly offset by reactivated customers. Product-level swings are noisy: in the four categories examined in detail, 105 of 190 declining products sold more again in September.

### 5. Reactivated customers cushioned the decline
From May to August, revenue from new customers fell $38.9K and from continuing customers fell $8.4K, while revenue from reactivated customers rose $21.5K, for a net change of −$25.8K. Continuing-customer revenue fell mainly in July and August, as the smaller June and July cohorts flowed through.

### 6. September's recovery was volume-led, and its quality varied by customer type
Revenue rose 20.8% to $141,240, within 1.0% of May. Active customers rose from 3,303 to 4,089 and transactions from 4,648 to 5,732, while ATV slipped from $25.15 to $24.64.

Share of September's customers still active in October, November and December:

| September group | October | November | December |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| New (1,625) | 19.0% | 16.2% | 18.3% |
| Continuing (1,246) | 60.8% | 58.5% | 58.9% |
| Reactivated (1,218) | 36.0% | 33.7% | 34.9% |

Revenue held through the fourth quarter, but new-customer arrivals fell back to 1,359, 1,185 and 1,202, against 2,200–2,600 a month from February to May. Reactivated customers grew from 11% of May revenue to 34% of December revenue. **September restored revenue without restoring acquisition.**

## Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Diagnose the fall in new-customer acquisition.** Examine channels, traffic, conversion, promotions and assortment for May to August. | New customers fell 59.6% and never returned to their earlier level |
| **2** | **Improve early-life retention.** Focus on first-to-second purchase conversion, onboarding and repeat-purchase incentives. | Month-1 retention was 14–18% in the decline cohorts versus 28–29% for March and April |
| **3** | **Protect continuing customers and build reactivation programs.** | Continuing customers retained about 59–61%, and reactivated customers now supply about a third of revenue |
| **4** | **Review the highest-impact declining categories**, after testing whether they simply reflect lost new customers. | New-customer loss was the largest component in LPF, 1EO and J4R, and product-level swings are noisy |
| **5** | **Monitor leading indicators** (table below). | These moved before and alongside the revenue decline |

### Suggested early-warning KPIs
| KPI | Healthy reference | Latest observed |
|---|---|---|
| New customers per month | 2,200–2,600 (Feb to May) | 1,202 (December) |
| Month-1 retention of new cohorts | 28–29% (March and April cohorts) | 14–19% (June to September cohorts) |
| Reactivated customers per month | 465 (May) | 1,481 (December) |
| Reactivated share of revenue | 11% (May) | 34% (December) |
| Retention of continuing customers | about 59–61% | about 59–61% |

## Limitations

- **One year of data.** A seasonal summer dip cannot be separated from a real acquisition problem.
- **"New" means first observed purchase.** The data starts on January 2, 2016, so January customers are not truly new.
- **No cause data.** There is no marketing, traffic, inventory, pricing or promotion data, so the analysis shows where the shortfall came from, not why. Confirming causes would require that data.
